## 1789-1815: Early republic, Quasi-War, Barbary Wars, War of 1812

- No navy at all until 1797: the Continental Navy was disbanded in 1785; the Naval Act of 1794 funded six frigates (first launched 1797); Navy Department created 30 Apr 1798.
- Army: ~700-840 regulars in 1789; Legion of the United States 1792-96; War of 1812 regulars authorized ~35,000 plus state militia (often unwilling to fight outside their states).
- Main offensive tools: frigates and sloops (single-ship actions), privateers, a small Marine Corps (1798), embargo (1807-09), and invasion of Canada (failed 1812-14).
- Wars: Northwest Indian War (1785-95), Quasi-War with France (1798-1800, undeclared), First Barbary War (1801-05), War of 1812 (1812-15), Second Barbary War (1815).
- Constraint: Congress declares war and grants letters of marque; the President cannot raise forces without appropriations.

## 1815-1860: Distant-station squadrons, Mexican War

- Navy organized as regional squadrons (Mediterranean, West India, Pacific, Brazil, East India, Home, Africa) - see fleets list.
- Ships of the line built (Act of 1816) but never fought a fleet battle. Steam enters (USS Fulton 1837, USS Princeton 1843).
- Army: ~6,000-16,000 regulars; Mexican War (1846-48) ~100,000 served incl. volunteers; Scott's Veracruz landing (Mar 1847, ~10,000 men) was the first large U.S. amphibious operation.
- Gunboat diplomacy: Perry opens Japan (1853-54); punitive raids (Sumatra 1832, Barrier Forts China 1856, Fiji 1855/1859).
- Indian removals and wars (Seminole Wars 1816-58) are the bulk of Army combat.

## 1861-1865: Civil War

- Union Army ~1 million at peak (1865); ~2.1M served. Conscription from 1863.
- Navy grows from ~42 commissioned ships to ~670 vessels; ironclads (Monitor, Mar 1862); Anaconda blockade proclaimed 19 Apr 1861.
- First U.S. air arm: Union Army Balloon Corps (1861-Aug 1863) for observation only.
- Total-war methods: Sherman's march (1864), Sheridan in the Shenandoah. Lieber Code (1863) - first codified U.S. law-of-war instructions.
- GAME NOTE: largely an internal war; foreign-attack options limited by need to keep Britain/France neutral (Trent Affair 1861).

## 1866-1897: Reconstruction, Indian Wars, the 'New Navy'

- Army capped at ~25,000 enlisted (1876); fighting on the Plains (Little Bighorn 1876, Wounded Knee 1890).
- Navy decays post-war to a few dozen obsolete ships (often described as smaller than Chile's in 1880s - approx.), then rebuilds: ABCD steel ships authorized 1883; Naval War College 1884; Mahan's 'Influence of Sea Power' 1890; first battleships commissioned 1895.
- Squadrons: European, North Atlantic, South Atlantic, Pacific, Asiatic (1868).
- Overthrow of the Hawaiian monarchy (1893) aided by Marines from USS Boston.

## 1898-1913: Spanish-American War and imperial era

- Spanish-American War (Apr-Aug 1898): Dewey's Asiatic Squadron at Manila Bay (1 May), Sampson/Schley at Santiago (3 Jul); Army expands to ~275,000 incl. volunteers.
- Philippine-American War (1899-1902, insurgency to ~1913); Boxer Relief (1900, ~2,500 U.S. troops); Banana Wars begin (Panama 1903, Cuba 1906, Nicaragua 1912).
- Great White Fleet (16 battleships, Dec 1907-Feb 1909); first dreadnoughts commissioned 1910; first submarine USS Holland (1900); Army buys first airplane 1909, Navy 1911.
- Roosevelt Corollary (1904) frames the Caribbean as a U.S. police zone.

## 1914-1918: WWI (U.S. belligerent Apr 1917-Nov 1918)

- Veracruz occupation (1914) and Punitive Expedition into Mexico (1916-17, ~10,000 troops, 1st Aero Squadron).
- Army ~4 million mobilized; AEF ~2 million in France; first U.S. tanks (Tank Corps, Renault FT) and Air Service (~45 front-line squadrons by Nov 1918).
- CHEMICAL: AEF Gas Service (Aug 1917), Chemical Warfare Service (28 Jun 1918), 1st Gas Regiment; U.S. forces used gas in 1918 (legal - no ratified ban then). U.S. suffered ~70,000 gas casualties (approx.).
- Navy: Battleship Division Nine with the British Grand Fleet; convoy escort; North Sea Mine Barrage (1918).
- Economic: Trading with the Enemy Act (Oct 1917).

## 1919-1938: Interwar

- Naval arms control: Washington Treaty (1922) and London Treaties (1930, 1936) cap battleships/carriers. First carrier USS Langley 1922; Lexington/Saratoga 1927.
- Geneva Protocol signed 1925 but not ratified (Senate) until 1975 - chemical stockpile retained. Kellogg-Briand (1928). Neutrality Acts (1935-37) restrict arms sales to belligerents.
- Army small (~120,000-190,000); Marines fight Banana Wars to 1934 (Nicaragua, Haiti); Fleet Marine Force 1933 and amphibious doctrine 1934.
- Air Corps (1926) develops strategic bombing doctrine; B-17 enters service 1938. Good Neighbor Policy (1933) ends Caribbean interventions.

## 1939-1945: WWII (U.S. belligerent Dec 1941-Sept 1945)

- Pre-war economic warfare: export controls on Japan from Jul 1940, asset freeze 26 Jul 1941, de facto oil embargo Aug 1941; Lend-Lease (Mar 1941); undeclared naval war in the Atlantic (1941).
- Peak ~12 million in uniform (1945); 16 million served. Navy: 6,768 ships of all types by Aug 1945 (NHHC force levels); numbered fleets created 15 Mar 1943.
- New options: carriers as capital ships, strategic bombing, airborne divisions, Rangers, OSS (13 Jun 1942), unrestricted submarine warfare, radio-guided bombs (Azon 1944), helicopters (1944).
- NUCLEAR: Manhattan Project; Trinity 16 Jul 1945; Hiroshima 6 Aug, Nagasaki 9 Aug 1945. Stockpile at end of 1945: 2 (DOE).
- CHEMICAL: large stockpile, FDR no-first-use pledge (Jun 1943), never used in combat. BIOLOGICAL: offensive program begins 1943 at Camp Detrick.

## 1946-1961: Early Cold War

- National Security Act 1947: Air Force, CIA (18 Sept 1947), NSC. Strategic Air Command (1946). Covert ops authorized by NSC 10/2 (1948): Iran 1953, Guatemala 1954, Bay of Pigs 1961.
- Nuclear stockpile: 299 (1950) -> 2,422 (1955) -> 18,638 (1960) -> 22,229 (1961). Thermonuclear test Ivy Mike 1952; Soviet first test 1949 ends U.S. monopoly.
- Delivery: B-36, B-47, B-52 (1955); Atlas ICBM on alert Sept 1959; first Polaris SSBN patrol 15 Nov 1960; Regulus, Matador, Snark cruise missiles; Thor/Jupiter IRBMs in UK/Italy/Turkey.
- Korea (1950-53) under UN mandate; massive retaliation doctrine (1954). Supercarrier Forrestal (1955); USS Nautilus (1955); USS Enterprise CVN-65 (1961).
- Special Forces (1952); helicopters in Korea. Chemical: sarin production 1950s. Bio: production at Pine Bluff from 1954. First ASAT test (Bold Orion, Oct 1959).

## 1962-1975: Vietnam era

- Cuban Missile Crisis (Oct 1962): quarantine, SAC at DEFCON 2, Jupiter withdrawal deal. Stockpile peaks at 31,255 (1967).
- Triad matures: 1,054 ICBMs and 41 SSBNs by 1967; Minuteman III (1970, MIRV), Poseidon (1971).
- Vietnam: peak 543,400 troops (Apr 1969); Rolling Thunder (1965-68), Arc Light B-52s, Linebacker I/II (1972, laser-guided bombs); airmobile 1st Cavalry Division; herbicides (Ranch Hand 1962-71); recon drones (1964-75); Haiphong mining (1972).
- Arms control: LTBT 1963, Outer Space Treaty 1967, NPT 1970, SALT I and ABM Treaty 1972. Nixon renounces biological weapons and chemical first use (25 Nov 1969). Geneva Protocol and BWC ratified Jan 1975.
- Constraints: War Powers Resolution (Nov 1973) - 60/90-day clock; Church Committee (1975); draft ends 1973. Mayaguez incident (May 1975). ASAT: Program 437 Thor (to 1975).

## 1976-1991: Late Cold War

- Stockpile ~24,000 (1980) -> 22,217 (1989) -> 19,008 (1991). New systems: ALCM (1982), Tomahawk (1983), GLCM and Pershing II in Europe (1983), Peacekeeper (1986), B-1B (1986), Trident I (1979) and II (1990), Ohio SSBNs (1981+), secret F-117 (1983).
- Treaties: SALT II (1979, unratified, observed to 1986); INF (1987, in force 1988) eliminates intermediate missiles; START I signed 31 Jul 1991. Sept 1991 Presidential Nuclear Initiatives withdraw most tactical nukes.
- Ops: Desert One (1980, failed), Lebanon (1982-84), Grenada (1983), Libya El Dorado Canyon (1986), Tanker War/Praying Mantis (1988), Panama (1989), Gulf War (1991, ~540,000 deployed, first Tomahawk combat).
- Delta Force (1977), SOCOM (1987), Goldwater-Nichols (1986). Navy peaks near 594 ships (1987) under the 600-ship goal.
- Chemical binary production 1987-90; May 1991 U.S. forswears all chemical use. ASAT: F-15 ASM-135 kills Solwind satellite (Sept 1985). EO 12333 (1981) bans assassination; Iran-Contra (1986).

## Option detail (scale and historical use)

### Infantry (regulars, militia, volunteers, National Guard) (`land_infantry`, 1789-now)

- **Scale:** 1789: ~700-840 regulars authorized. War of 1812: regular army authorized ~35,000 (actual peak ~38,000) plus militia. Mexican War: ~100,000 served. Union Army: ~1 million under arms at peak (1865), ~2.1M served. 1866-97: ~25,000 regulars (statutory cap 1876). 1898: ~275,000 incl. volunteers. WWI: ~4 million mobilized, ~2 million sent to France. WWII: Army+AAF ~8.3 million peak, 16 million served in all services. Korea ~330,000 peak in theater (approx). Vietnam 543,400 peak in-country (Apr 1969). Gulf War ~540,000 deployed. Iraq ~170,000 peak (2007), Afghanistan ~100,000 (2010-11). Active Army today ~450,000 (approx).
- **Historical use:** Every major U.S. war from the Northwest Indian War (1785-95) onward.
- **Legal status:** 1789-now: legal (Subject only to the law of war (Hague 1899/1907, Geneva Conventions 1949, UN Charter 1945 jus ad bellum).)

- **Scale:** Nov 1918: ~45 AEF squadrons, ~740 aircraft at the front (approx). WWII: AAF ~79,000 aircraft (1944 peak, approx). Today: ~several thousand combat aircraft across USAF/USN/USMC.
- **Historical use:** Every war from WWI on.
- **Legal status:** 1909-now: legal (Subject only to the law of war (Hague 1899/1907, Geneva Conventions 1949, UN Charter 1945 jus ad bellum).)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Air strikes are the default 'limited' coercive option post-1945; cost scales with civilian casualties and sovereignty violation.

### Strategic bombers (`air_strategic_bombers`, 1938-now)

- **Scale:** WWII: thousands of heavy bombers. SAC late 1950s: ~1,800-2,000 bombers incl. ~1,300+ B-47s (approx). Today: ~76 B-52H, ~45 B-1B, ~19-20 B-2A (approx).
- **Historical use:** Strategic bombing of Germany and Japan (incl. Tokyo firebombing 9-10 Mar 1945), Korea, Vietnam (Arc Light, Linebacker II Dec 1972), Gulf War, Kosovo (B-2 combat debut 1999), Afghanistan, Iraq, Midnight Hammer (B-2s with GBU-57s on Fordow/Natanz, 22 Jun 2025), Epic Fury (Iran, from 28 Feb 2026).
- **Legal status:** 1938-1977: legal (No treaty banned area bombing in WWII; Roosevelt's 1939 appeal against bombing civilians was a policy statement only.); 1977-now: restricted (Additional Protocol I (1977) codifies distinction/proportionality; U.S. not party but accepts much as custom. Bombers count under SALT II/START/New START.)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Bomber deployments are a visible escalation signal; use against cities carries extreme norm cost post-1945.

### Precision-guided munitions (`air_pgm`, 1944-now)

- **Scale:** Gulf War: ~8% of munitions dropped were guided; by Iraq 2003 ~two-thirds (approx).
- **Historical use:** WWII (limited), Vietnam, every conflict since 1991.
- **Legal status:** 1944-now: legal (Subject only to the law of war (Hague 1899/1907, Geneva Conventions 1949, UN Charter 1945 jus ad bellum).)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Lowers civilian-casualty cost per strike.

### Stealth aircraft (`air_stealth`, 1983-now)

- **Scale:** 59 F-117As built; 21 B-2s built.
- **Historical use:** Panama 1989, Gulf War 1991, Kosovo 1999 (one F-117 lost), Iraq 2003, Iran 2025-26.
- **Legal status:** 1983-now: legal (Subject only to the law of war (Hague 1899/1907, Geneva Conventions 1949, UN Charter 1945 jus ad bellum).)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Enables strikes with low risk of U.S. losses.

### Drones / UAVs (reconnaissance, then armed) (`air_drones`, 1964-now)

- **Scale:** Hundreds of MQ-1/MQ-9; ~14,000 drone strikes in Pakistan/Yemen/Somalia/Afghanistan 2004-2020 by independent counts (Bureau of Investigative Journalism; approx).
- **Historical use:** Targeted killing campaigns (Pakistan from 2004), Iraq/Syria vs ISIS, Soleimani strike (3 Jan 2020), Caribbean/Pacific strikes on alleged drug boats (from Sept 2025).
- **Legal status:** 1964-now: legal (Subject only to the law of war (Hague 1899/1907, Geneva Conventions 1949, UN Charter 1945 jus ad bellum).)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Low U.S. risk; persistent legal controversy over strikes outside declared war zones.

### Early cruise missiles (nuclear-armed, 1950s-70s) (`missile_cruise_early`, 1954-1976)

- **Scale:** Snark: one squadron; Hound Dog: ~600 built (approx).
- **Historical use:** Deterrence only.
- **Legal status:** 1954-now: legal (Subject only to the law of war (Hague 1899/1907, Geneva Conventions 1949, UN Charter 1945 jus ad bellum).)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Nuclear.

### ICBMs (`missile_icbm`, 1959-now)

- **Scale:** 1,054 ICBMs (1,000 Minuteman + 54 Titan II) from 1967; 400 single-warhead Minuteman III today.
- **Historical use:** Never used in combat.
- **Legal status:** 1959-1972: legal; 1972-2026: restricted (SALT I froze U.S. ICBM launchers at 1,054; START I/New START numerical limits.); 2026-now: restricted (No numerical treaty cap after 5 Feb 2026; NPT obligations remain.)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Use = general nuclear war. Test launches are routine and pre-notified.

### SLBMs (`missile_slbm`, 1960-now)

- **Scale:** Today ~970 warheads on SLBMs (FAS estimate, Jan 2025).
- **Historical use:** Never used in combat.
- **Legal status:** 1960-1972: legal; 1972-2026: restricted (SALT I / START / New START limits.); 2026-now: restricted (No numerical cap after New START.)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Nuclear.

### Theater/intermediate-range ballistic missiles (`missile_theater_ballistic`, 1958-now)

- **Scale:** Pershing II: 108 launchers in W. Germany.
- **Historical use:** Deterrence only. Jupiter withdrawal from Turkey was the secret quid pro quo of the 1962 crisis.
- **Legal status:** 1958-1988: legal; 1988-2019: treaty_banned (INF Treaty banned U.S./Soviet ground-launched missiles 500-5,500 km; U.S. eliminated 846 by 1991.); 2019-now: legal (U.S. withdrew from INF on 2 Aug 2019.)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Forward deployment near Russia/China provokes strong reaction (1983 Euromissile crisis; 2024 Typhon protests by China).

### Modern cruise missiles (conventional and nuclear) (`missile_cruise_modern`, 1982-now)

- **Scale:** ~2,000+ Tomahawks fired in combat since 1991 (approx).
- **Historical use:** Gulf War (~288 TLAMs), Iraq 1993/1996/1998, Sudan/Afghanistan (Aug 1998), Kosovo, Afghanistan 2001, Iraq 2003 (~800), Libya 2011, Syria (Shayrat Apr 2017: 59; Apr 2018), Houthis 2024-25, Iran 2025-26.
- **Legal status:** 1982-1988: legal; 1988-2019: restricted (INF banned ground-launched cruise missiles (BGM-109G GLCM eliminated by 1991); sea/air-launched allowed.); 2019-now: legal
- **Diplomatic cost:** Standard 'punitive strike' option of the 1990s+; low U.S. risk.

### Hypersonic boost-glide weapons (conventional) (`missile_hypersonic`, 2026-now)

- **Scale:** One battery = 4 launchers, 8 missiles. Expect 1-3 batteries by 2030 (projection, UNCERTAIN).
- **Historical use:** None.
- **Legal status:** 2026-now: legal (Subject only to the law of war (Hague 1899/1907, Geneva Conventions 1949, UN Charter 1945 jus ad bellum).)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Ambiguity (conventional vs nuclear) creates escalation risk with Russia/China.

### Nuclear weapons - strategic strike (`nuclear_strategic`, 1945-now)

- **Scale:** Stockpile (NNSA/DOE, end of FY; excludes retired awaiting dismantlement): 1945: 2 | 1950: 299 | 1955: 2,422 | 1960: 18,638 | 1962: 25,540 | 1967 peak: 31,255 | 1975: 27,519 | 1980: 24,104 | 1989: 22,217 | 1991: 19,008 | 1995: 10,904 | 2001: 10,526 | 2005: 8,360 | 2010: 5,066 | 2015: 4,571 | 2020: 3,750 | 2023: 3,748. FAS Jan 2025: ~3,700 stockpile, ~1,770 deployed (400 ICBM, ~970 SLBM, ~300 bomber bases, ~100 in Europe), ~1,477 retired awaiting dismantlement. Pre-1962 figures from the 1993 DOE release.
- **Historical use:** Hiroshima (6 Aug 1945) and Nagasaki (9 Aug 1945) - the only combat use ever. Considered/threatened in Korea (1950-53), Taiwan Strait (1954-55, 1958), Berlin, Cuba 1962 (DEFCON 2), 1973 (DEFCON 3).
- **Legal status:** 1945-1970: legal (No treaty restricted possession or use.); 1970-2026: restricted (NPT (in force 5 Mar 1970) recognizes U.S. as a nuclear-weapon state with Art. VI obligations; SALT I (1972), START I (1994-2009), SORT (2003-11), New START (2011-26) caps.); 2026-now: restricted (New START expired 5 Feb 2026 with no successor; Russia offered to observe limits if U.S. reciprocates, Trump sought a 'new, improved' treaty. No binding numerical cap as of late 2026 (verify for current status).)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Catastrophic: first use since 1945 would break the 'nuclear taboo'; expect allied rupture, global condemnation, retaliation risk from 1949 (USSR test) onward and assured destruction from the mid-1960s.

### Nuclear weapons - tactical / non-strategic (`nuclear_tactical`, 1952-now)

- **Scale:** Peak ~7,000 U.S. tactical warheads in Europe (early 1970s, approx); non-strategic weapons down >90% since 1991 (NNSA).
- **Historical use:** None in combat.
- **Legal status:** 1952-1991: legal; 1991-now: restricted (1991 Presidential Nuclear Initiatives withdrew Army ground-launched and most naval tactical nukes; only B61 bombs (~100 in Europe, NATO nuclear sharing) remain. Not covered by any treaty.)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Same nuclear taboo; 'limited' use risks escalation to strategic exchange.

### Nuclear test (signaling option) (`nuclear_test`, 1945-now)

- **Scale:** 1,054 U.S. tests 1945-1992 (DOE count).
- **Historical use:** Signaling throughout the Cold War.
- **Legal status:** 1945-1963: legal (Atmospheric testing 1945-62 (Nevada, Pacific; Castle Bravo 1954).); 1963-1992: restricted (Limited Test Ban Treaty (1963) bans atmospheric, underwater, space tests; Threshold Test Ban (signed 1974, in force 1990) caps at 150 kt.); 1992-now: restricted (Unilateral moratorium since Oct 1992 (last test 'Divider', 23 Sept 1992); CTBT signed 1996, Senate rejected 1999. Oct 2025 presidential directive to begin testing 'on an equal basis' - implementation unclear (UNCERTAIN). Underground test would require months to ~3 years of preparation per public NNSA statements (approx).)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Post-1992: high; would likely trigger Russian/Chinese resumption and NPT backlash.

### Chemical weapons (`chemical`, 1917-2023)

- **Scale:** Declared stockpile ~30,000 metric tons of agent (OPCW/ACA, 'nearly 30,000'); ~90% destroyed by 2012 at earlier sites, final ~10% at Pueblo (done Jun 2023) and Blue Grass (7 Jul 2023).
- **Historical use:** WWI only (1918). Not used in WWII, Korea, or later (except herbicides/riot-control agents in Vietnam, which the U.S. maintained were not chemical weapons).
- **Legal status:** 1789-1917: legal (Not stockpiled. U.S. declined to sign the 1899 Hague Declaration on asphyxiating gas projectiles (Mahan). 1907 Hague IV bans 'poison or poisoned weapons'. Not a practical option.); 1917-1918: legal (Used in WWI after Germany's 1915 first use: AEF Gas Service (Aug 1917), 1st Gas Regiment, Chemical Warfare Service created 28 Jun 1918; Edgewood Arsenal production.); 1919-1975: restricted (Geneva Protocol signed 1925 but NOT ratified by U.S. until 1975. FDR's June 1943 no-first-use pledge; large WWII stockpile, never used in combat. Sarin produced 1950s, VX 1960s. Nixon Nov 1969 renounced first use of lethal/incapacitating agents. Herbicides (Agent Orange, 1962-71) and riot-control agents used in Vietnam - contested under the Protocol.); 1975-1997: restricted (Geneva Protocol ratified 22 Jan 1975 (bans first use; U.S. reserved retaliation). Possession legal. Binary munitions produced 1987-1990. 1990 U.S.-USSR destruction agreement; May 1991 Bush forswore use for any reason including retaliation.); 1997-2023: treaty_banned (CWC in force 29 Apr 1997 (U.S. ratified 25 Apr 1997): use, production, stockpiling banned; stockpile under verified destruction.); 2023-now: destroyed (Last U.S. declared chemical munition (sarin M55 rocket) destroyed 7 Jul 2023 at Blue Grass, KY; OPCW verified. Option no longer exists.)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Pre-1925 moderate; 1925-1997 severe norm violation; post-1997 not available. GAME: model as 'restart a banned program' requiring CWC withdrawal (90-day notice, Art. XVI) and multi-year rebuild (designer estimate, not a public figure).

### Biological weapons (`biological`, 1943-1969)

- **Scale:** Small stockpile of standardized agents (details beyond scope).
- **Historical use:** Never used in combat.
- **Legal status:** 1789-1943: legal (No U.S. program; not a practical option.); 1943-1969: legal (Offensive program at Camp (later Fort) Detrick from 1943; production at Pine Bluff Arsenal from 1954; Geneva Protocol not ratified by U.S. Agents standardized but never used in war.); 1969-1975: restricted (Nixon renounced all biological warfare 25 Nov 1969 (toxins added Feb 1970); stockpile destroyed 1971-73.); 1975-now: treaty_banned (BWC signed 10 Apr 1972, ratified and in force for U.S. 26 Mar 1975. Defensive research only. No U.S. stockpile since the early 1970s.)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Post-1975: 'restart a banned program' - requires BWC withdrawal (3-month notice, Art. XIII) or covert violation; designer suggestion: multi-year lead time and extreme diplomatic/domestic cost. The U.S. does NOT have bioweapons today.

### Offensive cyber operations (`cyber_offensive`, 2007-now)

- **Scale:** 133 Cyber Mission Force teams (~6,200 personnel; full operational capability 2018).
- **Historical use:** See examples.
- **Legal status:** 2007-now: legal (No treaty; UN GGE norms (2015) and the non-binding Tallinn Manual apply international law. NSPM-13 (2018) eased presidential approval.)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Usually deniable; attacks on civilian infrastructure (power grid) carry high escalation cost and invite retaliation.

### Anti-satellite (ASAT) / counter-space (`space_asat`, 1959-now)

- **Scale:** A handful of operational launchers at any time; today ~a dozen-plus mobile jammers planned (Meadowlands target 32, approx).
- **Historical use:** Tests only; no combat ASAT use.
- **Legal status:** 1959-1967: legal; 1967-2022: restricted (Outer Space Treaty (1967) bans nuclear weapons in orbit and on celestial bodies; ASAT not banned. Congress barred ASAT tests against objects in space 1985-88.); 2022-now: restricted (U.S. unilateral moratorium on destructive direct-ascent ASAT missile tests (announced 18 Apr 2022); non-destructive counter-space (jamming) fielded.)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Destructive debris-producing ASAT tests now draw broad condemnation (UNGA 2022 resolution).

### U.S. Space Force operations (`space_force`, 2019-now)

- **Scale:** ~10,000 Guardians (approx).
- **Historical use:** Support to operations (e.g., Midnight Hammer cited by USSF).
- **Legal status:** 2019-now: restricted (Outer Space Treaty constraints.)
- **Diplomatic cost:** See ASAT.

### Embargo, trade war, financial sanctions (`econ_embargo_sanctions`, 1794-now)

- **Scale:** Dollar dominance makes post-1945 financial sanctions the most-used U.S. coercive tool.
- **Historical use:** See examples. Jefferson's 1807 embargo hurt the U.S. more than Britain; the 1941 oil embargo precipitated Pearl Harbor.
- **Legal status:** 1794-now: legal (Domestic authority: Embargo Act (Dec 1807), Non-Intercourse Act (1809), Trading with the Enemy Act (6 Oct 1917), OFAC (Dec 1950), IEEPA (1977). Unilateral sanctions raise WTO/third-party objections.)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Low-to-moderate; allies resent secondary sanctions; overuse erodes dollar leverage (game: diminishing returns).

### Covert action - early equivalents (`covert_pre1942`, 1790-1941)

- **Scale:** Ad hoc agents; tiny budgets.
- **Historical use:** Barbary regime-change attempt (1805), Civil War espionage, Banana Wars intrigue.
- **Legal status:** 1790-1941: legal (Contingent Fund of Foreign Intercourse (1790) let presidents spend secretly.)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Low if not exposed.

### Covert action - OSS / CIA (`covert_oss_cia`, 1942-now)

- **Scale:** OSS ~13,000 personnel at peak.
- **Historical use:** See examples.
- **Legal status:** 1942-1974: legal (OSS 13 Jun 1942-1 Oct 1945; National Security Act (26 Jul 1947) created CIA (18 Sept 1947); NSC 10/2 (Jun 1948) authorized covert operations.); 1974-now: restricted (Hughes-Ryan (1974) requires presidential findings; Church Committee (1975); EO 11905 (1976) and EO 12333 (1981) ban assassination; Intelligence Oversight Act (1980) requires congressional notification.)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Moderate-to-severe when exposed (Bay of Pigs, Iran-Contra 1986); domestic political cost after 1975.

### Restart a chemical weapons program (banned) (`chemical_restart`, 2023-now)

- **Scale:** No stockpile exists. Game suggestion: multi-year lead time before any usable capability; this is a designer estimate, not a published figure.
- **Historical use:** None.
- **Legal status:** 2023-now: treaty_banned (Requires withdrawal from the CWC (90 days' notice, Art. XVI) or covert violation.)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Extreme: pariah status, OPCW referral, allied rupture, domestic opposition (Congress funds would be required).

### Restart a biological weapons program (banned) (`biological_restart`, 1975-now)

- **Scale:** No stockpile since the early 1970s. Game suggestion: multi-year lead time; designer estimate only.
- **Historical use:** None.
- **Legal status:** 1975-now: treaty_banned (Requires BWC withdrawal (3 months' notice, Art. XIII) or covert violation.)
- **Diplomatic cost:** Extreme; arguably worse than chemical. Should be near-impossible to approve domestically.

## Uncertainties and caveats

- Squadron start/end dates are approximate; squadrons were frequently renamed, merged and suspended (esp. 1861-65). Atlantic Fleet formation date (1905/1906) varies by source.
- Eighth Fleet end date and Fifth Fleet WWII disestablishment date (1946 vs 1947) vary by source.
- Dark Eagle (LRHW): the Army said in Mar 2026 the first battery would receive operational missiles 'soon'; this file treats 2026 as first availability - verify.
- Stuxnet/Olympic Games attribution to the U.S. rests on press reporting (NYT 2012); never officially acknowledged.
- War of 1812 privateer numbers and prize counts vary widely across sources.
- Post-Feb 2026 nuclear arms-control status and Operation Epic Fury aftermath may have changed after April 2026 sources.
- Oct 2025 nuclear-testing directive: whether any explosive test preparation began is unclear in public sources.
- Near-future dates (Sentinel, Columbia, LRSO, CCA, CPS, F-47) are program targets that have repeatedly slipped.
- Chemical/biological 'restart' lead times are designer suggestions, not published estimates.
- Some per-unit counts (WWII aircraft, Vietnam helicopters, current inventories) are rounded approximations.
